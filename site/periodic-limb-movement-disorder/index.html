Periodic limb movement disorder (PLMD)

Repeated leg movements during sleep can fragment your rest without you ever knowing. A sleep study is the only way to see them.

Book an appointmentWhatsApp us

What is PLMD?

Periodic limb movements are repetitive twitches or jerks, usually of the legs, that happen every 20 to 40 seconds during sleep, often in clusters lasting minutes to hours. The big toe may extend, and the ankle, knee and hip may bend. When these movements are frequent and they disturb sleep or cause daytime problems that nothing else explains, this is periodic limb movement disorder.

Signs to look out for

  • Unrefreshing sleep and daytime tiredness with no obvious reason
  • A bed partner complaining of being kicked
  • Tangled or kicked-off bedclothes in the morning
  • Waking often in the night

Most people with PLMD are unaware of the movements. They are common in people with restless legs syndrome, and are also seen with sleep apnoea, narcolepsy, low iron, kidney disease and some antidepressants.

Diagnosis

PLMD can only be diagnosed with an overnight sleep study. Sensors on the shins count the movements per hour and show whether they wake the brain. The study also checks for sleep apnoea, which can trigger similar leg movements, since treating the apnoea often settles them.

Treatment

  • Checking and correcting iron levels
  • Reviewing medicines that can make movements worse
  • Treating any underlying sleep disorder, such as sleep apnoea
  • Good sleep habits, and less caffeine and alcohol
  • Medication when movements clearly disturb sleep and other steps have not helped

Frequently asked questions

How is PLMD different from restless legs?

Restless legs is an urge to move felt while you are awake, mostly in the evening. PLMD is movement that happens while you are asleep. Many people have both.

Do I need treatment if I have the movements but feel fine?

Not usually. Treatment is for movements that disrupt your sleep or your daytime functioning.
