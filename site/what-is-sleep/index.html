Sleep guide

What is sleep, and how much do you need?

Sleep is not switching off. It is an active, carefully organised process that restores the body and brain every night.

The stages of sleep

Through the night you move through two main kinds of sleep, which a sleep study can see clearly in your brain waves:

StageWhat happens
N1 (light sleep)The drift from wakefulness into sleep. Lasts a few minutes; you wake easily.
N2Heart rate and temperature fall. Takes up about half of a typical adult's night.
N3 (deep sleep)Slow-wave sleep: the most physically restorative stage, and hardest to wake from. More of it early in the night.
REM (dreaming sleep)The brain is very active and most vivid dreams happen, while the body's muscles are temporarily relaxed. Important for memory and mood. More of it towards morning.

Sleep cycles

These stages repeat in cycles of roughly 90 minutes, four to six times a night. Early cycles hold more deep sleep and later cycles more REM sleep, which is why cutting the night short costs you mostly dreaming sleep. The chart on our home page shows what this looks like in a real recording.

How much sleep do you need?

  • Adults: seven hours or more a night, regularly
  • Teenagers: eight to ten hours
  • School-age children: nine to twelve hours
  • Younger children and babies: more, including naps

Quality matters as much as quantity. Eight hours in bed is not restful if your breathing stops dozens of times an hour or your legs keep waking you.

Why sleep matters

Good sleep supports memory, concentration, mood, the immune system, blood pressure and blood sugar control. Long-term poor sleep is linked to heart disease, diabetes, weight gain, depression and accidents.

Simple habits that help

  • Get up at the same time every day, weekends included
  • Get daylight in the morning, and keep evenings dim
  • Keep caffeine to before midday, and limit alcohol near bedtime
  • Keep the bedroom cool, dark and quiet, and screens out of bed
  • Wind down for an hour before bed

When to see us: if you snore loudly, stop breathing in your sleep, struggle to sleep for weeks at a time, or feel sleepy during the day despite enough time in bed. Book an assessment.
