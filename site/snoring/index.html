Sleep conditions

Snoring: find the cause, then fix it

Snoring keeps partners awake and is sometimes the first sign of sleep apnoea. A proper assessment tells you which kind you have.

Book an appointmentWhatsApp us

Why do people snore?

Snoring is the sound of soft tissue vibrating as air squeezes through a narrowed airway during sleep. The narrowing can come from the nose (a deviated septum, allergies or polyps), the throat (large tonsils, a long soft palate or a bulky tongue), or simply from muscles relaxing more during deep sleep, after alcohol or with weight gain.

Simple snoring or sleep apnoea?

Many people snore without any harm to their health. The concern is when snoring comes with breathing pauses. Book an assessment if your snoring comes with any of these:

  • Pauses in breathing, choking or gasping, noticed by a partner
  • Daytime sleepiness, or nodding off when driving
  • Morning headaches or waking unrefreshed
  • High blood pressure that is hard to control
  • Snoring in a child, especially with mouth breathing or restless sleep

How we assess snoring

We start with a consultation and an examination of the nose and throat to see where the narrowing is. If there is any suggestion of sleep apnoea, an overnight sleep study measures exactly what happens to your breathing and oxygen levels while you sleep.

Treatment options

  • Lifestyle changes: weight loss, avoiding alcohol in the evening, and sleeping on your side often make a real difference.
  • Treating the nose: managing allergies or nasal blockage, or ENT surgery where there is a structural cause.
  • Oral appliances that hold the jaw forward during sleep.
  • CPAP if snoring turns out to be sleep apnoea.

Be wary of anti-snoring sprays and gadgets bought before you know the cause: they can quieten the noise while a breathing problem carries on underneath.

Frequently asked questions

My partner says I snore but I feel fine. Should I worry?

Not necessarily, but ask your partner whether you stop breathing or gasp. If you do, or if you are often tired in the day, get assessed.

Can snoring be cured?

Often it can be greatly reduced once the cause is clear, whether that is nasal blockage, weight, sleeping position or the shape of the airway.
