Narcolepsy: when sleep takes over the day

Narcolepsy is a lifelong neurological condition that is often missed for years. The right tests give a clear answer and a way forward.

Book an appointmentWhatsApp us

What is narcolepsy?

Narcolepsy is a disorder of the brain's control of sleep and wakefulness. The boundaries between being awake, deep sleep and dreaming (REM) sleep become blurred, so sleep can break into the day and dream-like experiences can happen while you are awake. It often begins in the teens or twenties, and is frequently mistaken for laziness, depression or simply being tired.

Symptoms

  • Excessive daytime sleepiness: a constant, overwhelming need to sleep, with sudden "sleep attacks" even in the middle of activities
  • Cataplexy: sudden, brief muscle weakness triggered by strong emotion such as laughter, from drooping eyelids or a sagging jaw to buckling knees (this occurs in type 1 narcolepsy)
  • Sleep paralysis: being briefly unable to move or speak when falling asleep or waking
  • Vivid hallucinations as you fall asleep or wake up
  • Broken night-time sleep, despite the daytime sleepiness

How it is diagnosed

Diagnosis needs a careful history and specialist sleep testing. This usually means an overnight sleep study to rule out other causes of sleepiness, such as sleep apnoea, followed by a daytime nap test (the multiple sleep latency test, or MSLT) that measures how quickly you fall asleep and whether you go straight into REM sleep. We will explain which tests you need and how to prepare, including any medicines to pause beforehand.

Living with narcolepsy

There is no cure yet, but treatment controls symptoms well for most people. It usually combines medicines that improve alertness or reduce cataplexy with planned short naps, regular sleep times, and practical support for work, study and driving safety.

Frequently asked questions

Is narcolepsy the same as being very tired?

No. Many conditions cause tiredness. Narcolepsy is a specific neurological disorder, diagnosed with sleep testing.

Can I drive if I have narcolepsy?

Not while your sleepiness is uncontrolled. Talk to your specialist about when driving is safe once treatment is working.
