Overnight sleep study (polysomnography) in Nairobi

The most complete test of your sleep: one comfortable night in our sleep laboratory, monitored from lights-out until morning.

What is a sleep study?

Polysomnography records what your body does while you sleep. It is the gold-standard test for sleep apnoea and is used to investigate narcolepsy, limb movement disorders, unusual behaviours in sleep and unexplained daytime sleepiness.

What it measures

SensorWhat it tells us
Brain waves (EEG)When you are asleep, and in which sleep stage
Eye movements (EOG)When you enter REM (dreaming) sleep
Chin and leg muscles (EMG)Muscle tone, teeth grinding and leg movements
Airflow and breathing effortPauses and shallow breathing, and whether they are obstructive
Oxygen level (oximetry)How far oxygen drops during breathing events
Heart rhythm (ECG)Heart rate and rhythm changes during sleep
Body position and snoringWhether problems are worse on your back, and how loud you snore

Who needs one?

  • Loud snoring with pauses, gasping or choking
  • Daytime sleepiness that does not improve with more sleep
  • Suspected narcolepsy or sleep attacks
  • Kicking or jerking legs at night
  • Unusual behaviour in sleep, such as acting out dreams
  • Setting the right pressure for a new CPAP machine

How to prepare

  • Keep to your usual routine on the day, and avoid napping
  • No caffeine after midday, and no alcohol on the day of the study
  • Wash and dry your hair; skip oils, gels and hair extensions or braids that cover the scalp if you can (tell us if not)
  • Remove nail polish from at least one finger for the oxygen sensor
  • Take your usual medicines unless we tell you otherwise, and bring a list of them

What to bring

  • Comfortable two-piece night clothes
  • Toiletries and anything you need for the morning
  • Your medicines and medication list
  • Your ID, insurance card and any referral letter or earlier results
  • Your own pillow, if it helps you sleep

On the night

You arrive in the evening and settle into a private room. A sleep technologist fits the sensors, which takes about 45 minutes and does not hurt, and checks the signals before lights-out. They monitor you through the night from the next room, and can help if you need the bathroom. In the morning the sensors come off, and you can go home or straight to work.

Results

A full night produces hours of recording that are scored and reported by a specialist. We book a follow-up appointment to explain your report, including your apnoea–hypopnoea index (AHI) where relevant, and agree a treatment plan.

Cost and insurance

Call or WhatsApp us for the current price. Many insurers cover sleep studies, sometimes with a doctor's referral. We can help you check your cover and request pre-authorisation before your study.

Frequently asked questions

Does a sleep study hurt?

No. The sensors are stuck to the skin with gentle tape or paste, with a soft band around the chest and a small sensor on a fingertip. Nothing pierces the skin.

What if I can't sleep with the sensors on?

Almost everyone sleeps enough for a useful result. We need a few hours of sleep, not a perfect night. The room is private and quiet, and you can use the bathroom during the night.

How much does a sleep study cost, and does insurance cover it?

Call or WhatsApp us for the current price. Many medical insurers cover sleep studies with a doctor's referral, and we can help you check your cover and get pre-authorisation before your night.

Do I need a referral?

You can book a consultation directly. Some insurers ask for a doctor's referral for the sleep study itself, so check with your insurer or ask us.

Can my child have a sleep study?

Children who snore, stop breathing or sleep very restlessly can be assessed. Call us to arrange the right appointment for your child's age; a parent stays with the child overnight.

When will I get my results?

Several hours of recording have to be analysed and reported by a specialist, so results are not ready the same morning. We will book a review appointment to go through the report and your treatment options with you.
